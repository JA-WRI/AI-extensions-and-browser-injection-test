A Beginner's Guide to Growing Tomatoes at Home

Tomatoes are one of the most rewarding plants for a first-time gardener. They grow quickly, produce fruit for months, and taste far better than most store-bought varieties. With a sunny spot and a little attention, almost anyone can grow them in a backyard bed or even in a pot on a balcony.

Choosing a spot

Tomatoes need plenty of sunlight, ideally six to eight hours a day. Pick the brightest spot you have. If you are using containers, choose pots that hold at least five gallons of soil so the roots have room to spread and the soil does not dry out too fast.

Soil and planting

Use a loose, well-draining soil mixed with compost. Plant seedlings deep, burying up to two-thirds of the stem, because tomatoes grow extra roots along any buried stem. This gives the plant a stronger foundation and helps it take up more water and nutrients.

Watering

Water deeply but not too often. The goal is moist soil, never soggy. Watering in the morning helps the leaves dry during the day, which reduces the risk of fungal disease. Uneven watering is a common cause of cracked fruit and blossom-end rot.

Pruning and support

Most tomato plants benefit from a stake or cage to keep the fruit off the ground. Removing the small shoots that appear between the main stem and the branches, often called suckers, helps the plant put more energy into fruit rather than leaves.

Harvesting

Pick tomatoes when they are fully colored but still firm. Leaving them on the vine a little longer deepens the flavor, but ripe fruit also attracts birds and insects, so check your plants every day once they start to turn.
